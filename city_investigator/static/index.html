      A research aid, not an official determination. For flooding that threatens life, call 911; to report a problem, use 311.

      Data: NYC Open Data (311, DOB permits, PLUTO), FloodNet (NYU and CUNY), NYC Stormwater Flood Maps (Mayor's Office of Climate and Environmental Justice), Iowa Environmental Mesonet, Mapillary (CC BY-SA 4.0). Models: SAM 3.1 and Muse Spark by Meta.

      Valdrada, a 3D city of New York by Mehmet Kerem Turkcan, built from public records (open source, MIT). Some street details, such as sidewalk sheds, are generated, so this view shows the place, not the evidence. It runs on this computer (setup in its BUILDING.md, then npm run dev in its client folder); a blank view means it is not running. The run's records appear in it once City Investigator's add-on is installed (python -m city_investigator.valdrada_addon and Valdrada's folder): flood water, sheds with a permit valid on the photo's date, scaffolding the photo shows, unsafe façades. Valdrada's own sheds, placed at random, are taken away. After draws the proposals under What could be done as illustrations (rain gardens, the shed an unsafe façade must have, a young tree, or a label): not a design and not a prediction. Click inside, then move with W A S D and look with the mouse. Open in a new tab if the mouse does not engage.
